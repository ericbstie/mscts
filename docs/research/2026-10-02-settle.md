# Settle: when vanilla's status stops counting a closed Bot — 2026-10-02

Evidence behind #97 (a Group starts only after the previous Group's Bots have
left the server). `Bot.close()` closes the socket, and the server removes the
player later. The question was whether a status request can tell when that has
happened, or whether the status lags the removal as well. **It cannot lag more
than the removal itself: vanilla drops its cached status in the same call that
removes the player, and rebuilds it on that tick. Closed Bots were gone from the
status 10 to 197 ms after the close.**

Facts are **verified** in this container unless a line says *inferred* or *not
shown*: bytecode with `scripts/research/javap.py server <Class>` (26.3 server
jar), and live runs against vanilla 26.3 (the Reference), on a 4-CPU container
shared with other jobs. The live probes were scratch scripts (not committed) over
the committed `Bot`, `run_group` and the probe Group (`SETBLOCK_OBSERVED`).

## What the status says, and when it changes

(Javap, server `MinecraftServer`, `ServerGamePacketListenerImpl`,
`ServerHandshakePacketListenerImpl`, `ServerStatusPacketListenerImpl`,
`PlayerList`.)

- The status is **cached**. `MinecraftServer.status` is rebuilt in
  `tickServer`, right after `tickChildren` (bytecode offsets 118 to 146 of
  `tickServer`), when `now - lastServerStatus >= STATUS_EXPIRE_TIME_NANOS`, which
  is `5 * TimeUtil.NANOSECONDS_PER_SECOND` (5 s; the static initialiser, offsets
  30 to 37). Left alone, a status would show a join or a leave up to 5 s late.
- A status request is answered from that cache. `ServerHandshakePacketListenerImpl`
  builds the status listener with `MinecraftServer.getStatus()` (offset 49 of
  `handleIntention`), which returns the `status` field, and
  `ServerStatusPacketListenerImpl.handleStatusRequest` sends the `ServerStatus` it
  was given.
- **A leave drops the cache at once.** `MinecraftServer.invalidateStatus()` sets
  `lastServerStatus = 0` (the next tick then rebuilds). It is called by:
  - `ServerGamePacketListenerImpl.removePlayerFromWorld()`, at offset 11, the
    first thing it does after closing the chat chain. `onDisconnect` calls it
    (offset 28, right after logging "lost connection"), and so does
    `switchToConfig` (offset 6). It goes on in the same call to broadcast
    "multiplayer.player.left" and to remove the player from the `PlayerList`
    (`PlayerList.remove`, offset 69).
  - `PlayerList.placeNewPlayer`, at offset 461, the end of a join.
- So the count follows a removal on the tick that removes the player, when the
  connection is ticked inside `tickChildren` (before the rebuild), and a join the
  same way. `PlayerList.remove` itself does not call `invalidateStatus`.
- *Not shown by bytecode*: that a Bot's closed connection is noticed on the next
  tick and not sooner. The measurements below are the evidence for it.

## Live: from the Bots closing to a status with `players.online == 0`

Measured as the time from `Bot.close()` returning (the probe Group: from
`run_group` returning, which closes `watcher` and Control's `control`) to the
first status, on a fresh connection polled every 1 ms, that read
`players.online == 0`. 20 samples each, in milliseconds:

| Case | min | median | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| one Bot, joined and closed | 16.3 | 42.9 | 113.8 |
| the probe Group's two Bots | 9.6 | 81.8 | 197.3 |
| all 40 | 9.6 | 60.2 | 197.3 |

- Every first poll after a close still read the full count (1 for one Bot, 2 for
  the probe Group's pair, never 1: both leave in the same tick). Right after a
  join the status read the right count too (1 or 2). It went from the full count
  straight to 0, within 200 ms, and nowhere near the 5 s expiry: the cache is
  dropped on the removal, as the bytecode says.
- Removal takes a server tick or a few (50 ms each) after the close, and more
  with two Bots, so a Group that played right after one that joined Bots could
  still see them online. Before the wait existed, the probe Group followed by
  `status/basic` (two Instances of the test's own, 20 repetitions) gave
  `mismatch` for 10 of 20 `status/basic` Verdicts (`players.online`,
  `players.sample`). With the wait, 400 of 400 (20 runs of 20) were `match`.

## What mscts uses

- `mscts.settle.SETTLE_INTERVAL_S = 0.02`: a poll every 20 ms, under a tick. A
  poll costs about 2 ms.
- `mscts.settle.SETTLE_TIMEOUT_S = 2.0`: about ten times the worst sample
  (197 ms).
- A status that lagged a removal by more than the removal itself would have
  stopped the work (the status could not tell a Group when to start). It does
  not, by the bytecode and by the measurements above.

## Not shown

- Pumpkin and other Candidates: the wait asks only the status, and the status
  protocol is the same everywhere (ADR-0001). Pumpkin's candidate-tier tests pass
  with it; its timing to empty was not measured.
- The probe Group itself gave one `mismatch` once in 60 plays during the
  development of the wait (config-phase Divergences, `select_known_packs` and
  `update_enabled_features`, for the Bot `watcher`), not in `status/basic`. It
  did not recur in 400 plays of the final code. Not investigated here, and the
  Transcript was not kept.
