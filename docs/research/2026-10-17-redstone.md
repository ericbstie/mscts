# When redstone components switch, tick by tick — 2026-10-17

Evidence for #39, the `redstone` Groups. Facts are **verified** with `scripts/research/javap.py`
on the 26.3 server jar unless a line says *live* (two vanilla 26.3 Instances, 2026-10-17).

## Repeaters

- `RepeaterBlock.getDelay` is the `delay` property times 2: a repeater waits 2 to 8 game ticks.
- A repeater's `facing` points at its input: `DiodeBlock.getInputSignal` reads the block at
  `pos.relative(facing)`, and `updateNeighborsInFront` updates the one on the other side.
- `DiodeBlock.checkTickOnNeighbor`, from `neighborChanged`, returns at once while `isLocked`.
  Otherwise, if `powered` differs from `shouldTurnOn` and no tick is due this tick, it schedules
  one `getDelay` later: priority `EXTREMELY_HIGH` if the repeater faces into another diode
  (`shouldPrioritize`), else `VERY_HIGH` if it is powered, else `HIGH`.
- `DiodeBlock.tick` returns while locked. A powered repeater whose input is off turns off. One that
  is off turns on, and if its input is already off it schedules its next tick `getDelay` later,
  so a pulse shorter than the delay comes out as long as the delay.
- `RepeaterBlock.isLocked` is `getAlternateSignal > 0`, and `sideInputDiodesOnly` is true: only a
  repeater or comparator at a side locks it. `updateShape` sets the `locked` property from
  `isLocked`, for a neighbour on the other axis.
- *Live*, `redstone/repeater`, 2 plays: the repeaters with delays 1 to 4 turn on 2, 4, 6 and 8
  ticks after their inputs, and off as many ticks after the inputs go. The locked one turns on 2
  ticks after its lock goes. The 1-tick pulse into a repeater with a delay of 4 comes out 8 ticks
  long. Its `locked` property shows `false` from the start, though `/setblock` placed it `true`:
  `updateShape` sets it again. Both sides sent the same packets in the same order on every tick.

## Comparators

- `ComparatorBlock.getDelay` is 2. `checkTickOnNeighbor` schedules a tick 2 later, at priority
  `HIGH` if the comparator faces into another diode, else `NORMAL`.
- `ComparatorBlock.calculateOutputSignal`: a rear input of 0 gives 0, and so does a side input
  greater than the rear one. Otherwise subtract mode gives rear minus side, and compare mode gives
  the rear input.
- The output strength lives in `ComparatorBlockEntity`, not in a block state: only `powered` goes
  over the wire. The dust after a comparator shows the strength.
- A comparator's rear input is the `getAnalogOutputSignal` of a block that has one, such as a
  chest, read directly or through one conductor block.
- `AbstractContainerMenu.getRedstoneSignalFromContainer`: f is the sum of each slot's count over
  its stack size, divided by the number of slots; the signal is `Mth.lerpDiscrete(f, 0, 15)`,
  which is floor(f × 14) plus 1 if f > 0. A chest has 27 slots: 1 stack of 64 gives 1, 2 or 3
  give 2, and 4 give 3.
- *Live*, `redstone/comparator`, 2 plays: 2 ticks after their rear inputs, the compare one
  outputs 15 (dust 15, 14, 13) and the subtract one 1. 2 ticks after the side inputs go, the
  subtract one outputs 15. A chest filled with `/item replace` updates the comparator behind it:
  1 a stack of 64, 3 with 4 stacks and 0 empty, each 2 ticks after the change. Both sides sent
  the same packets in the same order on every tick.

## Torches

- `RedstoneTorchBlock`: `TOGGLE_DELAY` 2, `RECENT_TOGGLE_TIMER` 60, `MAX_RECENT_TOGGLES` 8,
  `RESTART_DELAY` 160.
- `neighborChanged` schedules a tick 2 later when `lit` equals `hasNeighborSignal` and no tick is
  due this tick. A torch on the floor reads the signal of the block below it
  (`level.hasSignal(below, DOWN)`).
- `RECENT_TOGGLES` is a static `WeakHashMap` from a level to a list of toggles, each a position
  and a game time. `tick` first drops toggles from the front while the oldest is more than 60
  ticks old. A toggle is recorded only when a torch turns off (`isToggledTooFrequently(level,
  pos, true)`). The 8th toggle recorded at a position plays level event 1502 there and schedules
  the torch's tick 160 later. A torch turns on only while fewer than 8 toggles are recorded at
  its position.
- *Live*, `redstone/torch`, 2 plays: the torch turns off 2 ticks after its block becomes a
  redstone block and on 2 ticks after it is stone again. Powered every 4 ticks for 2, the second
  torch turns off for the 8th time on the 30th tick of its window: level event 1502 and the
  torch's `lit=false` come in that tick. It stays off when its power goes, and lights again 160
  ticks later, on the 4th step after `tick step 155`. `tick step 155` only sets the ticks to run
  (`stepGameIfPaused`, docs/research/2026-10-03-tick-step.md), so it answers before they have
  run, and Control waits until `time query gametime` says they ran. The tick Marks count only
  the ticks stepped one at a time. Both sides sent the same packets in the same order on every
  tick.

## Observers

- `ObserverBlock.updateShape`: when the changed neighbour is in the observer's `facing` and
  the observer is not powered, `startSignal` schedules its tick 2 later, unless one is already
  scheduled.
- `ObserverBlock.tick`: a powered observer turns off; one that is off turns on and schedules
  its tick 2 later. Each time, it updates the block behind it (`updateNeighborsInFront`). So a
  pulse lasts 2 ticks.
- `BlockInput.place`, which `/setblock` uses, sets the placed state from
  `Block.updateFromNeighbourShapes` unless the flags have 16 (`UPDATE_KNOWN_SHAPE`). That calls
  the observer's own `updateShape` for each side, its `facing` among them, so an observer placed
  with `/setblock` pulses 2 ticks later.
- *Live*, `redstone/observer`, 2 plays: both observers built before the window are powered
  on its tick 2 and off on tick 4. The stone placed in front of the first one on tick 0
  starts no pulse of its own, since one is already due. The air that replaces it on tick 6
  powers it on ticks 8 to 10. The third observer, placed on tick 12, and the second one watch
  each other. They pulse in turn from tick 14 until the third goes on tick 24, and the second is
  off on tick 26. Both sides sent the same packets in the same order on every tick.

## Dust

- Dust has no delay. When its strength changes, `DefaultRedstoneWireEvaluator.updatePowerStrength`
  sets the new state with flag 2 at once and then updates its neighbours, in the same tick.
- `RedstoneWireBlock.getConnectingSide(level, pos, direction, canClimb)`: `canClimb` is true when
  the block above the dust is not a conductor. Climbing, a side whose block can hold dust and
  has dust above it is `up` if that block's face is sturdy, else `side`. Otherwise a side is
  `side` if the block there connects, or if it is not a conductor and has dust below it, and
  `none` if not.
- A dust left with no connection becomes a cross, all four sides `side`, unless it was a dot
  (`getConnectionState`, which checks `isDot`).
- *Live*, `redstone/dust`, 2 plays: a redstone block at the start of a line of 16 dust makes it
  15 to 1 on the next tick, and the 16th stays 0; one tick after it goes, all of it is 0. Each
  line's change came in one `section_blocks_update`. The lone dust's shape changed on the tick
  after each block placed beside it, on it or above it. Both sides sent the same packets in
  the same order on every tick.
